## Traffic signals at US urban intersections

Traffic signals at US urban intersections (MUTCD 2009 Part 4D/4E, state and city signal design manuals): pole position, mast arm vs post mounting, signal head count, facing and height, pedestrian signals and pushbuttons, plus the abandoned city condition. Mapped onto Decimation's BlockTrafficLights prop (docs/references/prop_geometry.tsv: meta 2 arm to +X/E, 3 to +Z/S, 4 to -X/W, 5 to -Z/N, arm cells 0..7, top 7.85 m). Which side of that model the lenses face was NOT checked [not verified]: confirm with a gallery shot before placing.

- Every signalized approach gets at least two primary signal faces for the through movement, and all primary faces should be on the FAR side of the intersection (beyond it, seen across the junction), not on the near corner.
  - blocks: Per approach (4 at a crossroads, 3 at a T): one signal pole on the far corner, carrying at least two heads that face that approach. No head on the near corner the driver is standing at. Two faces = the heads on the arm, or one on the arm plus one on the pole itself.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part4/part4d.htm; check: CORRECTED: Two primary faces for the through movement is a Standard (MUTCD 4D.11 para 01). But 'all primary faces should be on the far side' is Guidance only for approaches at 45 mph or more (4D.11 para 07 item 4). For slower approaches MUTCD only says far side overhead faces have been shown to be safer (Support para 06), and 4D.14 requires at least one, preferably both, primary faces 40 ft or more beyond the stop line. The Minecraft rule (all heads far side, none on the near corner) is still a valid choice, just stricter than the minimum. Note: NJDOT's own basic design adds a near side left overhead head (see fact 2). (https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part4/part4d.htm)
- US practice puts the primary head on the FAR RIGHT corner of each approach (Toronto calls the far right head the primary, far left or median the secondary; NJDOT uses a far side right overhead mast arm per approach). A Moreno Valley standard also puts a luminaire on each far right approach pole.
  - blocks: Traffic drives on the right. Northbound traffic (moving toward -Z) is served by the pole on the NE corner, eastbound (+X) by the SE corner pole, southbound (+Z) by the SW corner pole, westbound (-X) by the NW corner pole. So a 4 leg crossroads has exactly 4 poles, one on every corner, each serving the approach that arrives AT it from the opposite side.
  - source: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/90ee-ecs-specs-tcas-TTS-808.100-Sep2023.pdf; check: OK
- The mast arm runs from the corner pole out over the roadway the approaching traffic is about to enter, perpendicular to that traffic, and the heads hang over the lane centres facing the approaching drivers (NJDOT: far side head as close as possible over the centre of the lane; two lanes: one head over the lane line or preferably one per lane; 3 and 4 lanes: one head per receiving lane).
  - blocks: Arm direction for Decimation BlockTrafficLights (arm points along the meta's front): NE pole meta 4 (arm W over the north leg), SE pole meta 5 (arm N over the east leg), SW pole meta 2 (arm E over the south leg), NW pole meta 3 (arm S over the west leg). The lit lenses must face the approaching traffic: NE arm heads face S, SE face W, SW face N, NW face E. Lens side of the model relative to meta is [not verified]: if a gallery shot shows the lenses on the other side, the corner/meta pairing stays and only the model must be checked, never flip the arm into the oncoming lanes.
  - source: https://nj.gov/transportation/eng/documents/BDC/pdf/DMR-Sec12-rev20140328.pdf; check: OK
- Two primary faces on one approach must be at least 8 ft (2.44 m) apart horizontally, centre to centre.
  - blocks: Heads of one approach at least 3 blocks apart (2 blocks = 2 m is too close). 6 wide road (sidewalk 0..4, road 5..10, sidewalk 11..15): arm head over the approach lane centre at block 9, second face on the pole at block 12 (3 apart). 10 wide road (sidewalk 0..2, road 3..12, sidewalk 13..15): heads over the two lane centres at blocks 9 and 11 is only 2 apart, so use block 8 and 11, or 9 on the arm plus the pole head at 14.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part4/part4d.htm; check: OK
- Cone of vision: at least one, preferably both, primary faces lie within about 20 degrees left and right of the approach centreline, measured from a point 10 ft (3 m) behind the stop line.
  - blocks: Lateral limit = tan 20 deg x (distance from the stop line + 3) = 0.36 x d. At a 6 wide road the far pole is about 12 m past the stop line, so heads may sit up to about 5 blocks either side of the approach lane centre (block 9.5): pole at block 12 and arm heads over blocks 6..10 all pass. At a 10 wide road (about 16 m) up to about 7 blocks: pole at block 14 passes.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part4/part4d.htm; check: OK
- Primary faces stand no less than 40 ft (12.2 m) and no more than 180 ft (55 m) beyond the stop line, unless a supplemental near side face is added; Wisconsin recommends at least 50 ft for post mounted and 75 ft for mast arm heads. The 40 ft minimum is waived where the width of the intersecting street makes it physically impractical.
  - blocks: Chunk grid: far pole distance = stop line gap + crosswalk + crossing road + far crosswalk. 6 wide crossing road: about 1 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 11 to 12 blocks, just at the 12 block minimum, acceptable (narrow street exception). 10 wide crossing road: about 1 + 3 + 10 + 3 = 17 blocks, fine. Every intersection is within 55 blocks of its own pole, so near side supplemental heads are never needed: do not place them.
  - source: https://wisconsindot.gov/dtsdManuals/traffic-ops/manuals-and-standards/tsdm/06/06-01-02.pdf; check: OK
- Stop line 12 to 24 in wide, placed at least 4 ft in advance of the nearest crosswalk line at a controlled intersection; crosswalk at least 6 ft wide.
  - blocks: Along each approach: crosswalk band 2 blocks wide (3 on a 10 wide road) in line with the crossing street's sidewalk, then 1 block of plain road, then a 1 block stop line (white) across the approach lanes only (not the opposing lanes). The signal pole stands beside the FAR crosswalk, never on the near one.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part3/part3b.htm; check: OK
- Overhead signal heads: bottom of housing at least 15 ft (4.6 m) above the pavement, top no more than 25.6 ft (7.8 m). Post mounted heads not over the road: bottom 8 to 19 ft (2.4 to 5.8 m) above the sidewalk.
  - blocks: Overhead heads occupy road level + 5 to + 7 (bottom at 5 m, top below 7.8 m). Decimation's BlockTrafficLights draws its top at 7.85 m above its own block: placed on a sidewalk 1 block above the road its top sits at 8.85 m over the road, about 1 m too high but visually acceptable; never place it on a raised planter or a 2 high curb. A head mounted on the pole itself (vanilla or second prop): bottom at sidewalk + 3 (3 m), never at + 1 or + 2.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part4/part4d.htm; check: OK
- A 12 inch three section head with a 5 inch backplate border measures about 52 x 24 in (1.33 x 0.61 m); NCDOT lists 52.5 x 25.5 in, 60 lb. All signal faces should have backplates.
  - blocks: A head reads as about 1.3 blocks tall and 0.6 wide, so one head per lane, never two heads in one block column. If a head is ever built from blocks (no prop), use a 1 wide by 2 tall dark column (black wool / coal block face, or a dark stained clay) with the lens side toward the approach.
  - source: https://learn.econolite.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2022/09/Backplate-datasheet_traffic-2022.pdf; check: CORRECTED: Dimensions: the Econolite datasheet is image only and I could not read it, so the 52 x 24 figure is [not verified]. A search shows NCDOT plan sheets listing a 12 inch 3 section rigid mounted head with backplate at 52.5 x 25.5 in, 60 lb, and a 5 in border is confirmed by LA DOTD and Peek. So about 1.3 x 0.65 m holds. Backplates: MUTCD 2009 says backplates 'should' be used on all faces only for approaches at 45 mph or more. At lower speeds they 'should be considered' for glare or busy backgrounds. 'All signal faces should have backplates' therefore overstates the rule for city streets. The Minecraft rule (one head per lane, 1 wide by 2 tall) is unaffected. (https://xfer.services.ncdot.gov/DSPlan/2020%20Highway%20Letting/03-17-20/Plans%20and%20Proposals/GUILFORD_34840.3.4_U-2581BA_C204371/Individual%20Sheets/260%20Signal%20Plans/260_520_U2581BA_SIG-8.4.pdf)
- Pole location on the corner: centre of the pole at least 32 in (0.8 m) from the face of curb; steel standards preferably 10 ft, at least 5 ft from the curb face; never in the curb ramp, never obstructing the crosswalk; not on the curve of a tight corner (radius under 15 ft, under 30 ft with occasional trucks and buses). Moreno Valley: within 5 ft of the crosswalk or its extension, else add a pushbutton post; no median poles.
  - blocks: 5 wide sidewalk: pole in the 2nd sidewalk block from the curb (centre 1.5 m, passes 0.8 m; 3rd block = 2.5 m also fine), on the row that lines up with the outer edge of the far crosswalk or 1 block beyond it. 3 wide sidewalk: the 2nd block (centre 1.5 m). Never the diagonal corner cell (curb ramp and turning sweep), never inside the crosswalk band, never in the road or a median. Keep the pole cell free of benches, bins, street lights.
  - source: https://nj.gov/transportation/eng/documents/BDC/pdf/DMR-Sec12-rev20140328.pdf; check: OK
- Mast arms come in 5 ft increments (40, 45, 50 ft...); NJ uses aluminium standards for arms up to 25 ft and steel beyond. Arm length is chosen so the heads reach over the approach lanes.
  - blocks: Decimation BlockTrafficLights arm reaches 7 cells from the pole. 6 wide road, pole at block 12: the arm ends at block 5, the far curb, covering the whole road (both lanes). 10 wide road, pole at block 14: the arm ends at block 7, covering the approach half (blocks 8..12) exactly. Never put the pole further back than the 2nd sidewalk block or the arm stops short of the inner lane.
  - source: https://tn.gov/content/dam/tn/tdot/traffic-engineering/TOM%202007%20-%20Traffic%20Signal%20Design%20Guidance.pdf; check: OK
- Mounting types: pedestal (post at the roadside), span wire (heads hanging from wires between poles) and mast arm (rigid arm over the approach). Mast arms improve upstream visibility; replacing pedestals with mast arms cut crashes 25 % overall and 63 % right angle (Kansas City). Arm length can limit mast arms at very large intersections.
  - blocks: City crossroads: mast arm poles (BlockTrafficLights) on all 4 corners. A narrow one way side street or a street narrower than 6 may use post mounted heads only (BlockTrafficLightsMiddle, or a pole head at + 3) on the far right and far left corners. No span wire (no prop for it).
  - source: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/04091/11.cfm; check: OK
- Pedestrian signal head: bottom of housing 7 to 10 ft (2.1 to 3.0 m) above the sidewalk, positioned for maximum visibility from the BEGINNING of the crosswalk (so it stands at the far end, looking back across); WALK white walking person, DON'T WALK Portland orange upraised hand, countdown in orange beside the hand. Moreno Valley puts pedestrian heads on the same pole as the vehicle signal.
  - blocks: Two pedestrian heads per crosswalk, one at each end, each facing ACROSS the street toward the opposite corner (8 at a crossroads, 2 on every corner pole). On the NE pole: one facing W (for people crossing the north leg from the NW corner) and one facing S (for people crossing the east leg from the SE corner). Height: sidewalk + 2 to + 3 (bottom at 2 to 3 m). Mounted on the signal pole, never on a separate post when the pole is within 1 block of the crosswalk.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part4/part4e.htm; check: OK
- Pedestrian pushbutton: within 5 ft of the crosswalk line extended (the edge farthest from the intersection centre), 1.5 to 6 ft (0.5 to 1.8 m) from the curb edge (never beyond 10 ft), about 3.5 ft (max 4 ft) above the sidewalk, face parallel to the crosswalk it serves; two buttons on one corner ideally 10 ft apart, but may share a pole when space is tight.
  - blocks: Optional detail: a vanilla stone button on the side of the pole cell's neighbour at sidewalk + 1, in the 1st or 2nd sidewalk block from the curb, within 1 block of the crosswalk's outer edge, its face pointing along the crosswalk (button on the wall facing the street being crossed). Two per corner (one per crosswalk), on two faces of the same pole column is acceptable.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part4/part4e.htm; check: OK
- Protected left turn: a separate head of left arrows over the left turn lane, at least one third of the way into that lane from its right lane line, normally on the far right mast arm; two heads for that movement, one overhead.
  - blocks: Only on roads with a marked left turn lane (10 wide streets with a turn bay): one extra head on the arm over the turn lane's centre, i.e. the arm needs to reach the lane next to the centre line. 6 wide streets never get a left turn head.
  - source: https://nj.gov/transportation/eng/documents/BDC/pdf/DMR-Sec12-rev20140328.pdf; check: OK
- Signal poles are typically 30 ft tall where they carry street lighting, with a 15 ft luminaire arm, luminaire on each far right approach pole; internally illuminated street name signs hang on the mast arms.
  - blocks: At a signalized corner the signal pole replaces the corner street light: do not stand a BlockStreetLight within 2 blocks of a BlockTrafficLights pole [inferred from the combined pole practice]. Next street light along the sidewalk at its normal spacing, starting about 1 chunk half (8 blocks) from the corner.
  - source: https://moval.gov/city_hall/forms/TransportationEngineering/TESS-DesignGuidelines.pdf; check: OK
- Abandoned city condition: in hurricanes span wire signals twist and get damaged (Pinellas County: 'one or two span wires that are damaged... they get twisted'), while mast arms are built for 144 to 158 mph winds and stay up; Florida counties are replacing span wire with mast arms for that reason. With no power every head is dark.
  - blocks: Ruined districts: keep almost every mast arm pole standing (they outlast buildings), heads dark (no light emitting blocks at the heads). Damage is local: at most about 1 pole in 8 missing, and only where a car or truck wreck sits on that corner cell (it was hit), the pole cell then empty or a 1 block stump [inferred]. Never place a pole lying across the road (no prop for it).
  - source: https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2025/06/18/pinellas-county-moving-more-traffic-lights-off-wires-; check: OK

## US regulatory road signs

US regulatory road signs (MUTCD): STOP, speed limit, ONE WAY / DO NOT ENTER, parking signs. Position, height, offset, spacing and facing, translated to block rules. Numbers are from the 2009 MUTCD text on mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov; the 11th edition (2023) may renumber sections [not verified against 2023]. Coordinates: Minecraft x = east, z = south; "faces south" = the readable side points south (read by someone standing south of it). Facing metadata: Decimation BlockProp front 2 E, 3 S, 4 W, 5 N (docs/prop_placement.md); vanilla wall_sign 2 N, 3 S, 4 W, 5 E; vanilla standing_sign 0 S, 4 W, 8 N, 12 E, odd/even steps of 22.5 deg (standing sign values inferred from vanilla 1.7.10 ItemSign yaw math, [not verified in game]). Decimation has no US STOP / speed limit / parking sign model (prop_catalogue only lists BlockRussianRoadSign / Arrow / Warning, shop logo signs, BlockMaintenanceScreen), so US signs need vanilla signs on a post or a new model [inferred].

- STOP sign side: it shall be on the near side of the intersection, on the right hand side of the approach it controls, as close as practical to the intersection. A second STOP sign on the left side is optional (wide throat, multi lane approaches, or a median / island).
  - blocks: Right hand traffic: the sign stands on the curb to the driver's right, on the approach leg BEFORE the crossing. Northbound (moving -z) approach: east sidewalk (+x side), south of the intersection. Southbound: west sidewalk, north of it. Eastbound (moving +x): south sidewalk, west of it. Westbound: north sidewalk, east of it. Never on the far side of the intersection and never on the corner square itself. Optional left copy only on the 10 wide road (two lanes per direction): mirror it to the other curb of the same approach.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part2/part2b.htm; check: OK
- STOP sign orientation: signs are mounted vertically at right angles to the direction of, and facing, the approaching traffic (MUTCD 2A.20; PennDOT: STOP sign oriented at a right angle to approaching traffic). Where roads meet at an acute angle, angle or shield the sign so traffic it does not apply to cannot read it.
  - blocks: The face points back down the approach, opposite to the travel direction. Northbound approach: face SOUTH (Decimation meta 3, wall_sign 3, standing_sign 0). Southbound: face NORTH (Decimation 5, wall_sign 2, standing_sign 8). Eastbound: face WEST (Decimation 4, wall_sign 4, standing_sign 4). Westbound: face EAST (Decimation 2, wall_sign 5, standing_sign 12). A STOP sign must never face across the street (toward the road) or toward the intersection; that is the classic wrong turn. Unit check: on every approach the sign's face normal is the reverse of the lane's travel vector.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part2/part2a.htm; check: OK
- Two way vs all way stop corners (MUTCD intersection examples, minor crossroad case): with the east west road as the major road, the minor road's STOP signs stand on the northwest and southeast corners, facing north and south respectively. An all way stop puts one sign on the right of every approach.
  - blocks: Two way stop on a north south minor street: SE corner area (south leg, east curb, faces south) + NW corner area (north leg, west curb, faces north); nothing on the major street. All way stop at a 4 leg crossing: exactly 4 signs, one per leg, always on the leg's right curb: south leg east curb faces S, north leg west curb faces N, west leg south curb faces W, east leg north curb faces E. Rotational pattern: the 4 posts form a pinwheel, never two posts on the same corner diagonal pair facing the same way.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part2/fig2a_03_longdesc.htm; check: OK
- Urban STOP sign distance along the street: in the urban curb and sidewalk example the sign is at least 4 ft in advance of the crosswalk edge nearest approaching traffic and at least 2 ft from the road edge; at a wide throat intersection it may stand up to 50 ft back from the intersected road's edge. Section 2B.10: install it in advance of the crosswalk line nearest approaching traffic, not farther than 50 ft from the edge of the intersected roadway.
  - blocks: Count rows away from the intersection along the approach: crosswalk rows first, then ONE empty gap row (4 ft = 1.2 m, rounds to 1 block), then the sign row. So sign row = outermost crosswalk row + 2 (or a little more), never inside or touching the crosswalk. Hard limit: the post is no more than 15 blocks (50 ft) from the cross street's curb line. Default: same row as the stop line.
  - source: https://gis.penndot.pa.gov/BPR_PDF_FILES/Documents/LTAP/TechSheets/TS_211_Stop_Sign_Placement.pdf; check: OK
- STOP sign mounting height in urban areas with sidewalks, parking or pedestrians: minimum 7 ft from the sidewalk / ground to the bottom of the sign (rural 5 ft). With a plaque below, the 7 ft applies to the lowest sign. ADA headroom along circulation paths is 80 in; a sign with its bottom edge at 80 in or higher is outside the protrusion limit.
  - blocks: Bottom of the board at 2.13 m or more above the walking surface: post fills walking level y and y+1, sign board occupies the block at y+2 (board spans about y+2.0 to y+2.8; a 30 in sign is 0.76 m). Never put the board at y or y+1 on a sidewalk (it would also be a head height obstacle). Rural / wasteland roads without sidewalk: board may sit at y+1 (5 ft = 1.5 m).
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part2/part2a.htm; check: OK
- Lateral offset in curbed urban streets: recommended 2 ft from the face of the curb to the near edge of the sign; may be reduced to 1 ft in tight spots (PennDOT, MUTCD 2A.19 para 11). Minneapolis requires 1.5 ft from curb face to signs; Mountain View uses 18 in. Rural roads: 12 ft from the travel lane edge (6 to 12 ft at intersections).
  - blocks: Post goes in the sidewalk column next to the curb or the one after it, never in the road and never against the building line. 6 wide road + 5 wide sidewalks (road x 5..10, sidewalks 0..4 and 11..15 in the street chunk): post in the 2nd column from the curb (x 3 on the west side, x 12 on the east side) so the 1 block board edge is about 1 m from the curb. 10 wide road + 3 wide sidewalks (road 3..12, sidewalks 0..2 and 13..15): post in the curb column (x 2 / x 13), the constrained 1 ft case, leaving 2 columns clear for walkers.
  - source: https://gis.penndot.pa.gov/BPR_PDF_FILES/Documents/LTAP/TechSheets/TS_211_Stop_Sign_Placement.pdf; check: CORRECTED: PennDOT TS 211 (2 ft recommended, 1 ft tight), MUTCD 2A.19 Option 11 (at least 1 ft from face of curb where sidewalk width is limited), Minneapolis Street Design Guide 3.3F (1.5 ft from face of curb to signs) and MUTCD 2A.19 Guidance 03 (12 ft rural) are confirmed. The Mountain View 18 in claim could NOT be found; the nearest real sources are Woodside Fire (18 in from curb front to post centre, fire lane signs) and the NYC Street Design Manual (most street furniture 18 in from the curb). Also '6 to 12 ft at intersections' is the distance back from the intersecting road's travel way edge (Fig 2A-3 C), not a lateral offset. Minecraft column choices are consistent with 1 to 2 ft. (https://sdg.minneapolismn.gov/design-guidance/boulevards-and-furnishings/utilities-and-signs)
- Sidewalk clear path: the pedestrian access route needs at least 48 in (4 ft) continuous clear width excluding the curb, and street furniture may not reduce it. Signs, lamps, benches belong in the furnishing zone next to the curb, typically 3 to 5 ft wide (NYC Street Design Manual).
  - blocks: Sign posts share the curb side furnishing column(s) with street lights, benches and bins; keep at least 1 full block (better 2) of the sidewalk free along its whole length on the building side. On a 3 wide sidewalk, signs only in the curb column; on a 5 wide sidewalk, columns 1 and 2 from the curb may hold furniture, columns 3 to 5 stay clear. Do not put a sign post in the same block as a lamp post or bench; shift it 1 row along the street instead.
  - source: https://www.access-board.gov/prowag/proposed/chapter-r3-technical-requirements/; check: OK
- STOP line: solid white line across the approach lanes, 12 to 24 in wide, at least 4 ft in advance of the nearest crosswalk line at controlled intersections, and not more than 30 ft nor less than 4 ft from the nearest edge of the intersecting traveled way when there is no crosswalk.
  - blocks: One block wide white row, painted only across the approach half of the road: the 3 right hand blocks of the 6 wide road (northbound: x 8..10) or the 5 right hand blocks of the 10 wide road (northbound: x 8..12). It sits 1 empty block behind the crosswalk (crosswalk outer row + 2). Without a crosswalk: 1 to 9 blocks back from the cross street's curb line, default 2. The STOP sign post stands in the same row on the sidewalk.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part3/part3b.htm; check: OK
- Crosswalk markings: lines 6 to 24 in wide; a crosswalk marked by two transverse lines should be at least 6 ft wide (gap between lines at least 6 ft).
  - blocks: Crosswalk band 2 to 3 blocks deep across the full road width, aligned with the cross street's sidewalk band. Two line style: white edge rows with at least 2 road blocks between them; ladder / continental style: white bars along the walking direction across the 2 to 3 rows.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part3/part3b.htm; check: OK
- Sign free zone before crosswalks: in urban areas where crosswalks exist, signs should not be placed within 4 ft in advance of the crosswalk (MUTCD 2A.16). Signs should normally be on the right hand side of the roadway.
  - blocks: The single sidewalk row directly behind each crosswalk band (on every approach) stays free of all sign posts, so drivers see pedestrians stepping off. This is the same row as the stop line gap. Every regulatory sign defaults to the right hand curb of the traffic it serves.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part2/part2a.htm; check: OK
- STOP sign size: 30 x 30 in on single lane conventional approaches; a minimum of 36 x 36 in on multi lane approaches. Placing two STOP signs on one post is prohibited, and no sign may be mounted back to back with a STOP sign in a way that hides its octagon shape.
  - blocks: Board is at most 1 block wide (0.76 to 0.91 m) on both road types. One STOP board per post; never put a ONE WAY, street name or parking sign back to back with it (a street name blade ABOVE it on the same post is the normal US look). The 10 wide road (2 lanes per direction) uses the 36 in sign, still 1 block.
  - source: https://gis.penndot.pa.gov/BPR_PDF_FILES/Documents/LTAP/TechSheets/TS_211_Stop_Sign_Placement.pdf; check: OK
- Speed limit (R2-1) placement: shall be installed at the points of change from one speed limit to another, and additional Speed Limit signs shall be installed beyond major intersections; a sign showing the next limit at the downstream end of a zone. MUTCD gives no fixed repeat interval. Example in Figure 2A-2 C: urban Speed Limit sign bottom 7 ft above the sidewalk, near edge 2 ft from the pavement edge.
  - blocks: Per travel direction, on that direction's right curb, facing oncoming traffic (same facing table as the STOP sign: northbound faces S, etc.). Place one at each city entry (where the highway / edge ramp becomes a city street) and one just past every major intersection: in the first street chunk after the crossing, about 4 to 8 rows past the far crosswalk so it does not crowd the corner [inferred, MUTCD gives no distance]. Height and offset as the STOP sign (board at y+2, post in the furnishing column).
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part2/part2b.htm; check: OK
- Speed limit repeat spacing: practice varies by city; NACTO reports preliminary Seattle studies where raising sign density from one per mile to one every 1/4 mile lowered speeds and crashes. A simulator study found spacing had negligible effect but 20 percent of signs were not perceived, so repetition helps.
  - blocks: On long straight city avenues without major intersections, repeat a speed limit sign each direction about every 400 blocks (1/4 mile = 402 m, 25 chunks); in a dense grid the 'after every major intersection' rule already fires more often, so do not add interval signs within 200 blocks of the last one. Typical city value on the sign: 25 to 35 mph [not verified per city].
  - source: https://nacto.org/publication/city-limits/the-right-speed-limits/corridor-speed-limits/determine-best-option-for-speed-management/signs-markings; check: OK
- ONE WAY (R6-1 / R6-2) at unsignalized intersections: ONE WAY signs shall be placed parallel to the one way street at all alleys and roadways that intersect it, on the near right and the far left corners of the intersection, facing traffic entering or crossing the one way street. At a T intersection: near right and far side, facing the stem approach. With a median 30 ft or wider, near right and far left of each directional roadway; under 30 ft, the far right corner may get one too.
  - blocks: For each cross street direction, the drivers' near right corner and far left corner get a sign. For a north south cross street crossing an east west one way street, that is always the SE and NW corners (northbound: near right SE, far left NW; southbound: near right NW, far left SE), so each of those 2 corner posts carries two boards back to back, one facing N and one facing S. The board's long axis runs PARALLEL to the one way street (east west), and the arrow points in the one way travel direction (eastbound: arrow points east). Put the post on the corner square's curb column, outside the crosswalk band. Height board bottom at y+2. On a 1 street T: near right corner + the far curb opposite the stem, facing down the stem.
  - source: https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/htm/2009/part2/part2b.htm; check: OK
- DO NOT ENTER (R5-1, 30 x 30 in): placed directly in view where a road user could wrongly enter a one way roadway, mounted on the right hand side facing traffic that might enter in the wrong direction; a second sign on the left side is optional, especially where traffic approaches from an intersecting road. WRONG WAY (R5-1a, 36 x 24 in) goes farther along the one way roadway than the DO NOT ENTER sign.
